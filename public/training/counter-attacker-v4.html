Counter Small v4 adds opponent-aware shot selection and predicted attacking replies to a compact physics graph. This graph fits closely within the compute and operator budgets. It does not fit Nano. The same six observations and three action scores apply; all decisions remain inside the ONNX file.

The weights are constructed analytically. No gradients, optimizer, distillation, or mutation search were used. Match simulations were development and evaluation evidence. Pacer versus Apprentice remains the default matchup.

What it predicts

The earlier Handcrafted Nano and Small models calculate landing positions accurately, but their incoming targets do not adapt to the opponent’s position. Counter Small considers all 19 outgoing integer vertical velocities. Each angle corresponds to legal contact-center bands. It checks which bands remain reachable after each first action, forecasts the receiver’s recovery, and scores placement with smaller travel and own-recovery penalties.

While the ball travels away, it forecasts the opponent’s reachable extreme return heights. It moves toward the endpoint farther from the recovery point that the attacker expects it to approach. This models the attacker’s short-horizon heuristic; it does not solve multi-return optimal play.

Exact compact flight calculations, wall reflection, sparse ReLU tables, saturated Tanh integer selectors, and shared expressions keep the graph small. Incoming score terms are scaled onto an exact integer grid before maximum reduction, with a half-point stay preference for equal best scores.

Limits and numerical corrections

The graph retains ordinary two-unit movement parity and conservatively omits additional positions reachable through future wall clamps and reversals. Its flight and angle calculations use the current speed, but risk and recovery use cap-13 safety tables during early exchanges. Its outgoing forecast considers endpoints, while the actual attacker can choose another reachable shot.

Independent checks exposed numerical issues before final testing. A prototype stay penalty was smaller than float32 maximum-reduction noise. The final graph quantizes both contact-band ends to the movement lattice, preserves empty bands, and scales incoming scores by 2,000 so they remain exact integers. A half-point tie preference then selects stay without suppressing a strict integer score advantage. These were analytical corrections, not fitted weights.

All development screens and numerical failures are retained separately from the frozen final tests. Future model selection needs fresh reserved seeds.

Verified native and browser inference

Independent verification passed 30,224 numeric and action-boundary probes plus 52,326 lattice-band cases with zero intended-action errors. Six native matches contain 10,296 frames. Both the frozen v4 and live TypeScript engines reproduce every transition, with zero state or action mismatches. The ONNX regenerates byte-identically and frozen hashes remain intact.

Browser WASM parity replayed the same six matches with live Counter/Pacer scores and retained native adaptive-attacker controls. All 13,904 ONNX decisions retain their actions and all states match. Counter scores match native exactly on 12,296 decisions; Pacer’s maximum score difference is 7.63e−6 with no action changes.

Construction, numerical-fix history, all development outcomes, final raw results, frozen hashes, and compressed replays are retained in training/results/counter-attacker-v4/.
