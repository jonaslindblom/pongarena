PONGARENA / HANDWRITTEN CLASSIC V4

163 parameters.
Written by hand.

No training · 141 MACs · 128 held-out paired seeds

Quill Nano v4 is a plain 6→12→4→3→3 ReLU network. Its fixed formulas anticipate flight, reflect wall bounces and aim away from the opponent. It beats Pacer, Robust Strong and both larger Nano presets in this evaluation, with the same parameter count as Tracker.

Constructor, seeds and evidence ↓

What “handwritten” means

Every weight and bias is generated from an explicit mathematical formula. No gradient descent, supervised distillation, regression, evolutionary search, CMA, or automated parameter fitting was used. We inspected Tracker's weights and the Vector Nano and Prism Small constructors, then manually compressed the physical ideas. No learned tensors were copied.

Manual development used 32 seeds. The retained proposals were a short constant forecast, a clipped flight approximation, a square-curve flight approximation, and a refinement that uses five square segments. A final numerical safety adjustment widened positive relay offsets without changing any development match result. The early constant-forecast implementation also had a corrected intercept error. The model and the separate 128-seed list were hashed before the final evaluation; no weights changed afterward.

Parameters include every stored zero and bias: 84 + 52 + 15 + 12 = 163. The four dense matrices execute 72 + 48 + 12 + 9 = 141 MACs. The graph contains only four Gemm layers and three ReLUs, with no branches, shared weights, preprocessing or hidden physics program.

How the small network works

The first layer represents a piecewise-linear square curve at four fixed knots. The difference of two squares approximates distance multiplied by vertical velocity. Shifting distance to zero at our paddle preserves a useful contact-time anchor despite the coarse approximation.

The next layers gate the forecast when the ball travels away, fold a single wall bounce, subtract our paddle position and encode the opponent's side of center. The final three scores are negative target error, a 1.1-unit movement deadband, and positive target error. Opposite-end aiming follows Vector Nano's closed-form physical prior.

Quill uses the maximum horizontal speed of 13 to approximate flight time and a small discrete-contact correction. It cannot calculate exact flight time at every speed, plan multiple returns or reproduce the adaptive scripts' recovery choices. Far-away forecasts can cross more than one wall, beyond its accurate reflection range. Outgoing recovery stays near center with a small opponent-dependent offset.

Where it loses

Prism Small, Attacker and Defender remain stronger. Quill lost 760 rallies to Prism Small while winning 333. Attacker won 1,245 of 1,280 rallies; Defender won 1,013, with another 232 draws. Its small size and favorable learned-model matchups do not establish general tactical mastery or universal strength.

Reproduction and runtime checks

The constructor regenerates the exact deployed bytes. The active arena validator confirms the dense contract, all 163 parameters and 141 MACs. All 13,360 recorded frames reproduce in the live TypeScript engine, with zero action or state mismatches. Maximum score difference was 0.000010491, below the 0.00002 tolerance.

All 90 automated tests pass without skips, including checks for anticipation, wall reflection, opposite-side aiming, recovery and the movement deadband using the actual WASM network.
