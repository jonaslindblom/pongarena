A stronger trained Nano.
Still 163 parameters.

6→16→3 Tanh · 163 parameters · 144 MACs

Refined Nano improves the previous trained 163-parameter model from 26.62% to 30.46% mean rally score against six unchanged reference opponents. The improvement holds on an independent confirmation set. It remains weaker than Plain Nano 447 and, in a separate fresh comparison, handwritten Quill Plus.

The submitted player is an ordinary dense network with one hidden layer, six raw observations and three action scores. All weights and biases count toward the 163 parameters. There is no weight sharing, runtime augmentation or added physics logic.

The selected model was frozen before 256 paired seeds × both sides × five rallies = 2,560 rallies per matchup. Rally score counts a win as one and a draw as half. Each of the six opponents contributes equally to the field mean.

How the trained model was refined

The starting 6→16→3 Tanh actor came from the earlier distillation and depth study. This follow-up tested four CMA evolutionary-search arms, four PPO continuations and two warm-distillation arms, including a matched experiment with training-only vertical reflection. The extra PPO and distillation experiments were registered from development evidence before independent selection.

CMA restarted its search distribution around the current best weights every 16 generations. Each arm ran 64 generations with 20 proposals and 16 training seeds per generation. The selected checkpoint, cma-fine-g40, came from the small-perturbation arm with sigma 0.015 and equal opponent weights.

All 83 scheduled checkpoint entries—68 distinct tensor states—entered selection on 96 separate paired seeds. The highest field score was eligible only with a nonnegative direct margin against the starting model and no script decline over five percentage points; the unchanged control stayed eligible. PPO and the two distillation follow-ups did not produce the selected winner. Their negative results remain in the evidence.

Validation, provenance and limits

Both comparisons use frozen ONNX files, deterministic first-maximum action selection, unchanged Classic v4 rules and both paddle sides. Reported primary intervals use 10,000 bootstrap samples of whole paired seeds, preserving all rallies and opponents together. Raw evaluator rows retain their original 2,000-sample intervals; the confirmation-only correction and source hash chain are recorded.

Native ONNX and browser WASM checks agree on 4,096 action probes per model for Refined Nano, the starting 163 model and Plain Nano 447, with zero action mismatches. Independent audits reproduce every final game tally and primary confidence interval. Both final seed sets were checked against recorded historical IDs and known training intervals, including the Quill evidence bundles for the later comparison.

Historical seed logs are incomplete, and different seed IDs can generate the same opening. An earlier depth-study evaluation range overlapped an ancestral training interval; that does not establish exact seed reuse. These new results use separately audited seeds. Strength remains specific to the tested opponents, and the study does not establish an optimal architecture or a general limit of PPO.
