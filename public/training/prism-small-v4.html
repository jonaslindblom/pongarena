The ONNX graph is exactly Gemm → Relu → Gemm → Relu → Gemm. Each affine layer owns its fixed weights and biases, and the final layer directly emits the three linear action scores. All six raw observation dimensions enter the first dense layer. Every coefficient, including zeros, counts toward the budget. Small permits 2,048 parameters and 8,192 MACs.

The opponent-paddle column of the first weight matrix is all zero: the current contact branch does not adapt its aiming to the opponent’s position. Six-dimensional port compatibility does not mean every input is informative. Pacer versus Apprentice was the default matchup for this study.

These are hand-constructed physical priors in ordinary dense weights, with no formal training, optimizer, learned-source transfer, or inference helper. The active observation-6/action-scores-3/dense-mlp-v3 contract applies. Earlier general-graph planning programs remain retired archives.

Why this differs from Prism Nano

A regular square-ridge grid makes the two interpolation errors in the position/vertical-velocity interaction cancel for integer vertical velocities. A fixed timing offset matches the cap-speed trajectory lattice. Those cancellations are encoded in the dense coefficients themselves. With float32 weights the forecast is numerically approximate; lower horizontal speeds remain a cap-13 approximation.

The incoming target follows one connected family of contact placements. Half-integer goals admit two adjacent integer paddle centers within a 1.101-unit deadband, making the intended contact less sensitive to which two-unit movement parity the paddle follows. The folded target curve is represented by ordinary second-layer ReLU units.
