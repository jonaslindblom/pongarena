How the compact graphs play

Supported scalar MatMul products calculate flight distance and vertical travel. A reciprocal curve is exact at integer horizontal speeds 5–13; binary ReLU ramps implement the integer flight-time ceiling, and wall reflection gives the next contact height. Sparse piecewise-linear curves encode the target tables without storing dense matrices of zeros and ones.

Small retains each speed stage’s incoming contact targets and outgoing reply-coverage midpoints from the original handcrafted policy. Nano keeps cap-speed incoming placement and moves toward court center while the ball travels away. Both use a 1.1-unit movement deadband, preserving the intended integer/half-integer target actions while tolerating numeric error.

The contact target minimizes one-return recovery risk. It may be unreachable from the current paddle position, and it does not adapt shot placement to the opponent’s current position. Those limits matter against the stronger scripts.

Verified weights, retained evidence

Both exports pass 8,192 construction probes and 21,962 independent probes each, including target/deadband boundaries, with zero intended-action mismatches. Small matches the Open reference’s actions on every checked probe and encountered match decision. Both compact ONNX files regenerate byte-identically and pass zero-input import checks.

Twelve complete native matches contain 22,196 frames and 44,296 decisions. The frozen v4 and live TypeScript engines reproduce every transition with zero state or action mismatches. Signed-zero observation differences produce identical native scores and actions. Browser WASM parity also passed all twelve matches, 22,196 frames and 29,844 compact-model decisions, with zero score, action or state differences from the native replay.
