A physically derived court-end aim

The target uses the observed opponent-paddle side to aim toward the opposite court end. At cap speed the horizontal crossing takes 26 ticks; a continuous approximation to the return angle gives the following desired contact center:

h = predicted incoming landing height
D = 4 when opponent Y ≥ 80; otherwise 156
p = (83/78) × h − (5/78) × D

In normalized coordinates the same target is (83/78) × L + (19/312) × S, where S is −1 for integer opponent heights through 79 and +1 from 80 upward. ReLU responses encode the cue, with a continuous transition between those adjacent heights. This formula explains the fixed output coefficients; it is not an extra runtime operation.

The first hidden layer uses a coarser square-ridge grid, with step 4/9, to approximate anticipation. Its interpolation errors cancel on the cap-speed contact lattice. Away from contact, the full cap-speed forecast remains approximate, with about 1.5 court units of error in the construction diagnostic; lower speeds also remain approximate. While outgoing, the network returns toward center 80.

The court-end aim uses a continuous angle model. Actual returns quantize vertical velocity, paddle movement has a parity, and the desired center can be outside bounds or unreachable in the remaining flight. Opponent motion after observation is not planned. These limits remain even when the next-contact forecast is accurate.
